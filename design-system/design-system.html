Sheet 01 Visual language

A house, drawn in steel before it is lived in.

The working drawing for Halo — the practice that surveys, installs and commissions smart home systems. Warm off-white paper, one steel blue that does all the pointing, and a single light source from the top-left that gives every surface a little depth. Device cards are the exception: those stay drawn in hairlines, like the plan they came from.

Principles

Four rules. If a decision later feels arbitrary, it probably breaks one of them.

P—01

Paper first

Warm off-white is the default surface. Color is spent, not sprayed — steel blue marks what matters and nothing else.

P—02

Depth over borders

Structure comes from depth and alignment, not boxes. One light source, top-left, on every surface — no contradicting shadows.

P—03

Measured

Every dimension is a multiple of 4, every column of 24. The grid should be felt even when it is switched off.

P—04

Quietly technical

Annotations, tick marks and readings signal precision. Jura's geometry states it; the soft warm paper keeps it habitable.

Soft shadow only works on --paper-000 (#FDFCF8). On pure white the highlight vanishes and the surface collapses into a plain drop shadow — which is why the ground is warm off-white, and why nothing sits on a tinted background without being re-tuned.

Soft, and the exception

When a thing should be drawn instead

Soft — the default

Anything a person reads or acts on: cards, buttons, inputs, nav, tiles, panels. Raised when it responds to you, recessed when it contains something. Subtle enough to repeat forty times down a page without the layout going soft-focus.

Drawn — the exception

Anything that represents a real device or a plan: device cards, spec panels, annotated diagrams. Hairlines, 4px corners, no shadow. The contrast is the point — hardware on the page looks like hardware on a drawing.

Switches and sliders inside a .card--drawn revert to hairlines too, so a card never mixes the two languages.
